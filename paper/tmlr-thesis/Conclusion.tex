\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
The evaluation establishes that a single Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) policy can generalize to previously unseen network backbones and enhance the deployed baseline at the packet level. This policy is trained using an analytical model and evaluated at the packet level. The research shows that multi-agent reinforcement learning combined with a graph neural network (GNN) routing protocol outperforms existing routing protocols such as OSPF and single-agent policies. In overload scenarios, the policy reduced packet loss from 14.1\% to 2.9\% on GÉANT and from 27.3\% to 10.6\% on Germany50 compared to OSPF. Furthermore, the results show that MARL with GNN achieves superior performance on medium to large networks, such as GEANT and Germany50. However, on the smallest topology it does not improve on a correctly configured OSPF, and in the feasible regime the headroom it frees is paid for with a small increase in packet loss.

For future work, we recommend exploring larger training scales and trying a wider variety of network topologies and traffic patterns. The main limitation of this study was limited time and computing resources. Extending the duration of experiments and increasing compute power could lead to stronger results. Additionally, training the model at the packet level could further align the outcomes closer to real-world situations. This study uses the actor-critic Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm to calculate rewards in MARL, but it is necessary to explore alternative reward algorithms as well. Finally, evaluating the model on real hardware routers could produce the development of a production-ready model.
