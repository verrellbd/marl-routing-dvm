\section{Conclusion} 
Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for routing leverages rewards collected from dynamic network environments. In this work, performance was successfully measured using link utilization and end-to-end quality of service metrics. The pipeline, which involved training the function on an analytical surrogate and then evaluating it via ns-3 integration, enhanced the network's ability to learn from its environment.

This study demonstrated improved decision-making on three network topologies, which are Abilene, GEANT, and Germany50, when using MARL compared to OSPF. Employing a graph neural network (GNN) architecture, the model reduced packet loss under overload from $14.1\%$ to $2.9\%$ on GEANT and from $27.3\%$ to $10.6\%$ on Germany50. These results indicate effective collaboration between the architecture and the reward mechanism.

Overall, MARL reduced the bottleneck offered load by approximately $53\%$ on the large topology and $33\%$ on the medium-scale one, with a smaller $3\%$ gain on the small topology. It minimized maximum link utilization and enhanced quality of service by reducing packet loss and delay.
