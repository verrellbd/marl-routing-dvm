\begin{abstract}
An interconnection network (Internet) is a group of networks consisting of nodes carrying traffic between multiple sites. As traffic demand increases rapidly, network routing protocols must deliver efficient and adaptive solutions for packet transmission. This study introduces a routing approach for intra-domain networks that combines Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) with Graph Neural Networks (GNN) to improve Quality of Service (QoS). In this approach, each network node is modeled as an autonomous agent that utilizes a GNN-based state to capture both local and global network environment data. Nodes use a centralized training and decentralized execution model to optimize routing decision strategies. A single policy is trained on seventeen network topologies and evaluated, without retraining, on three it has never seen, using real measured traffic and packet-level NS-3 simulation. Under overload traffic, the approach outperforms the Open Shortest Path First (OSPF) routing protocol on two of the three evaluation networks, reducing packet loss from $14.1$--$27.3\%$ to $2.9$--$10.6\%$ at a comparable mean delay of $18.1$--$20.7$\,ms against OSPF's $19.7$--$21.4$\,ms. Where demand fits within capacity, it lowers maximum link utilization from $95.6$--$97.3\%$ to $66.7$--$70.8\%$ at a packet loss of $0.12$--$1.02\%$, freeing headroom that OSPF leaves unused. On the smallest network, whose link capacities are uneven, a correctly configured OSPF is not improved upon. Integrating MARL with GNNs therefore enhances quality of service on networks of uniform capacity and adapts to topologies unseen during training.
\end{abstract}
