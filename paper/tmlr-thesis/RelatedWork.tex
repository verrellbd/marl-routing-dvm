\section{Related Work} 
Network routing protocols are generally categorized as either intra-domain or inter-domain routing. The application and scope of these protocols vary depending on network usage. This study primarily focuses on intra-domain routing within telecommunication backbone networks, with particular emphasis on Open Shortest Path First (OSPF) \citep{rfc2328}. OSPF is a link-state protocol that rapidly discovers network topology within a domain to facilitate packet transmission. However, OSPF offers limited traffic control capabilities. Traffic engineering in OSPF is limited to tuning link cost weights. \citet{832225} demonstrated that optimally tuning OSPF link weights for a given demand matrix is nondeterministic polynomial-time hard and does not guarantee optimal routing. Equal-Cost Multi-Path (ECMP) additionally splits traffic across equal-cost paths but offers only coarse load balancing.

To optimize network routing through traffic engineering, researchers have investigated several intra-domain protocols. \citet{article} evaluated multiple intra-domain routing protocols that incorporate traffic engineering, including Multi Protocol Label Switching (MPLS), OSPF, and IP-based approaches. Their results indicate a trade-off between flexibility and scalability when employing traffic engineering. MPLS provides superior path control and supports arbitrary traffic splitting, whereas IP/OSPF offers advantages in scalability, reduced cost due to the absence of Label Switched Path (LSP) state, and automatic failure rerouting. To address these challenges, this study evaluates a recent protocol that employs machine learning as a baseline approach.

Recently, various machine learning approaches have been implemented in network routing. Reinforcement learning has emerged as one of the most prominent paradigms for this purpose, with agents learning through trial and error during training to maximize system rewards \citep{SHAKARAMI2020107496, sutton_reinforcement_2018}, following the widespread attention the paradigm gained from DeepMind's Atari-playing agent and AlphaGo \citep{shaheen2026reinforcementlearningstrategybasedatari}.

In network routing, reinforcement learning offers a novel approach to augmenting existing protocols such as OSPF. Several studies have explored the integration of reinforcement learning with neural networks to enhance routing performance. \citet{ALMASAN2022184} present a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agent integrated with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that generalizes across unseen topologies in optical networks. \citet{Yamin2024} implement Relational A2C for Integrated Access Backhaul (IAB) networks, leveraging relational base-station associations to group similar destinations. \citet{11364348} introduce MARINA, a cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework for Software-Defined Networks (SDNs) that simultaneously optimizes network usage and quality of service constraints. \citet{10225785} propose SR-LILLC, a MARL approach utilizing Deep Q-Networks (DQN) to reduce end-to-end delays in Segment Routing networks. \citet{11134361} develop a Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning method for dynamic routing in Mobile Ad Hoc Networks using Graph Neural Networks, providing a scalable and efficient solution for rapidly changing networks.

However, all of these works are evaluated on varied topologies and datasets. \citet{ALMASAN2022184} evaluate their DRL agent on 232 real-world network topologies from the Topology Zoo dataset, demonstrating strong performance on networks not encountered during training. \citet{11364348} assess their cooperative MARINA framework on the real-world GEANT2 network topology, showing that it satisfies significantly more quality of service (QoS) requirements than traditional protocols such as OSPF and ECMP. \citet{10293208} conduct simulation experiments using real-world topologies, including Abilene and GEANT, combined with measured traffic data to demonstrate the reliability of G-Routing.


Despite strong results, these approaches share limitations relevant to this study. Most are validated using analytical or flow-level models rather than packet-level simulation. Packet-level simulation captures realistic network dynamics that analytical and flow-level models abstract away, at higher computational cost \citep{1443314}. As a result, gains reported on analytical models may not translate to packet-level behaviors such as queueing, loss, and delay. Consequently, learned routing has primarily been applied to optical \citep{ALMASAN2022184}, SDN \citep{11364348}, segment-routing \citep{10225785}, or mobile ad-hoc networks \citep{11134361}, rather than to fixed telecommunication backbones evaluated on real measured demand at the packet level.

This work addresses that gap by evaluating Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Graph Neural Networks against the deployed baseline on fixed telecommunication backbones, using SNDLib topologies and real measured traffic \citep{SNDlib10, OrlowskiPioroTomaszewskiWessaely2010} and NS-3 \citep{Riley2010} for packet-level measurement.
