\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
This section presents evaluation results for the Abilene, GÉANT, and Germany50 topologies using real measured operator traffic. The results emphasize two performance indicators and the reward trade-offs the trained policy makes on networks it has not seen. For the reinforcement learning method, results are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation \emph{across model seeds}, each seed contributing its own mean over the retained matrices. 
Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} summarises all three metrics for the four methods across both regimes; the sections that follow examine each metric in turn.

The results are limited by the small training resources and evaluation set. We train policies on $1.5$ million environment steps with ten seeds, then test them on three backbone networks using nine matrices for each regime. The other gaps are also due to differences in link capacity: GEANT and Germany50 have uniform link speeds, but Abilene only has one different link capacity. MARL demonstrates adaptation to three new backbones, but we have not shown it can handle networks with different link capacities. Lastly, each simulation uses a fixed traffic matrix, and it has not yet been tested with other intra-domain routing such as MPLS or segment routing.

\subsection{Reward Trade-offs on Unseen Topologies}
\label{sec:res-reward}
The policy was trained on seventeen SNDLib topologies across ten different seeds, then evaluated on three new topologies. Details about training convergence are in Appendix ~\ref{app:training} and Table ~\ref{tab:reward-decomp} presents a comparison of the trade-offs among congestion, delay, and hop counts. On Abilene the policy detours on only $3.0\%$ of hops for a delay cost of $4.9$ and reduces the bottleneck to $100\%$ of OSPF's, and on GÉANT it detours on $4.4\%$ of hops for a cost of $9.8$ and reaches $69\%$; in both, the congestion term dominates the return and the delay penalty is small. Germany50 shows the largest improvement, to $48\%$ of OSPF's bottleneck, but there the delay penalty of $58.7$ is the larger of the two terms, bought with detours on $13.5\%$ of hops, and the overall return falls below the $-100$ that OSPF scores by construction. Therefore, the trade-off of the policies for unseen topologies results in larger topologies paying more in detours than they recover in congestion.

\subsection{Bottleneck Offered Load}
\label{sec:res-util}

Measured traffic data from SNDLib \citep{SNDlib10} provides actual traffic values across multiple timeframes. Traffic scaling is applied to generate demand for simulation. The resulting load is simulated on an analytical surrogate domain, since ns-3 link capacity is limited to 100\%. Therefore, simulation demand is evaluated under two distinct conditions: overload and feasible regimes. Results for both regimes are reported for all three networks.

Figure~\ref{fig:link-utilization} breaks the offered-load panel of Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} out by topology, with the underlying values listed in Table~\ref{tab:offered-util}. The results show that MARL consistently outperforms other methods in both overload and feasible regimes. MARL achieves superior performance compared to OSPF, ECMP, and single-agent approaches on the GEANT and Germany50 networks, although it performs slightly worse than the single-agent method on Abilene. Additionally, MARL demonstrates a significant reduction in maximum offered load, achieving up to a 52\% decrease on GEANT and Germany50 in both regimes. In the overload regime, MARL reduces the offered load to 97.7\%, maintaining it below the 100\% threshold.

In the feasible regime, OSPF reaches a maximum offered load of 81\% on Germany50, while ECMP achieves 60.2\%. ECMP works more efficiently than OSPF on both GEANT and Germany50, reducing the maximum offered load by about 15\%. On the other hand, single-agent shows significant improvement on Germany50 to 60\%, but underperforms on GEANT at 70\%. MARL has better performance than all three baselines on GEANT and Germany50, achieving 49.0\% and 38.9\%, respectively.

The overload regime produces similar results to the feasible regime. In this setting, both the single-agent and MARL methods improve routing decision performance. The single-agent approach beats OSPF and ECMP on Abilene and Germany50, with improvements of 2\% and 36\%. MARL also shows strong results, reducing the maximum offered load on GEANT and Germany50 to 97.7\% and 118.7\%, respectively.

These observations demonstrate that MARL improves routing by reducing the maximum offered load across both overload and feasible regimes. MARL also outperforms the single-agent approach on GEANT and Germany50. In two out of three networks, across both regimes, MARL distributes traffic more effectively than OSPF, ECMP, and single-agent methods. Therefore, the objective of using MARL to lower the bottleneck achieved.

\subsection{Packet-Level Quality of Service}
\label{sec:res-qos}
Quality of Service results were obtained using the NS-3 simulator. FlowMonitor was employed to collect data from this packet-level simulator. To ensure consistency, measured traffic from SNDLib was used under maximum offered load conditions. The results are divided into overload and feasible regimes to highlight differences between these conditions. Packet loss is reported as a percentage, and delay is measured in milliseconds (ms). Figures~\ref{fig:loss-qos} and~\ref{fig:delay-qos} shows the comparison between the topology.

Overall, MARL is the strongest method in the overload regime, leading on four of the six loss and delay measurements. It achieves the lowest packet loss and delay at GEANT and demonstrates superior delay performance at Abilene and packet loss at Germany50. MARL reduces packet loss to 3.36\% at GEANT. However, as shown in Figure \ref{fig:loss-qos} and Figure \ref{fig:delay-qos}, loss and delay results are similar across methods in the feasible regime, with both MARL and ECMP achieving a packet loss of 0.12\% at GEANT.

MARL demonstrates strong performance in the overload regime by outperforming other methods in four out of six metrics. Specifically, it reduces delay to 112.8 ms at Abilene and 18.1 ms at GEANT under bottleneck traffic conditions. MARL also achieves the lowest packet loss at Germany50, with a value of 10.63\%. In contrast, OSPF achieves the lowest packet loss at Abilene (14.35\%), and MARL achieves the lowest delay at Germany50 (17.1 ms).

In contrast, results in the feasible regime do not indicate significant differences in quality of service among the methods. Several metrics are identical across topologies and methods. For example, OSPF and ECMP achieve delays of 11.5 ms and 12.8 ms at Abilene, ECMP and MARL both record a packet loss of 0.12\% at GEANT, and OSPF and the single-agent method both achieve a packet loss of 0.03\% at Germany50. In this regime, packet loss varies only between 0.03 and 1.09\%, and delay ranges from 1.8 to 21.6 ms.

These differences are statistically supported. In the feasible regime MARL lowers the maximum link utilization on all nine paired matrices, by $19.1$ percentage points on average (Wilcoxon signed-rank, $W=0$, $p=0.004$). In the overload regime it lowers packet loss on seven of the nine, by $9.0$ points on average, and the rank test reaches the conventional threshold ($W=5$, $p=0.039$); the two matrices that favour OSPF are Abilene matrices. Restricted to GÉANT and Germany50 the improvement holds on all six matrices ($W=0$, $p=0.031$). The headroom result is therefore significant across every network tested, whereas the loss result is significant only where the decentralized formulation works at all.

Quality of Service measurements demonstrate how MARL outperforms during overload conditions. The observed results for delay and packet loss show that both measurements are influenced by the routing protocol, particularly when hops increase or bottleneck traffic occurs. We achieve the objective of this study, where MARL leads on four of the six loss and delay measurements under overload, with the finding that in the feasible regime the baseline routing protocols already provide sufficient performance.
